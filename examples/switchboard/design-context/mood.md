# Mood — Switchboard

*Not from a moodboard. Produced on 2026-08-16 from a forty-minute conversation with the two engineers who maintain Switchboard and one coordinator who has used it for three years, held with `trace.md` open on the table. Provisional until someone sits through a full shift; the coordinator's answers were about work, not about how anything looks, which is the right way round and also means this file is a translation.*

## In three lines

A good workbench. Everything to hand, nothing pretty, arranged by someone who does the work. It should feel like the tool is keeping up with you, and it should never feel like it is asking you to slow down and admire it.

## Qualities

- **Quick before comfortable** — where speed of scanning and comfort of reading conflict, scanning wins. This is the quality that most contradicts ordinary design instinct here, and it is the one to hold.
- **One meaning per signal** — a colour, a shape or a position says one thing across the whole product. The tool's real problem is not that it is plain, it is that it has stopped being readable at a glance.
- **Honest about consequence** — actions that cannot be undone should feel different from actions that can. Right now they feel identical, and that is the difference between a fast tool and a dangerous one.

## References

- A departures board: dense, glanceable, no ornament, and nobody has ever complained about its typography.
- A well-worn kitchen pass: everything within reach, arranged by frequency of use rather than by category.
- The current board on a quiet Tuesday, which several people described without prompting as "fine, actually". The product is already close.

## Anti-references

- NOT a modern SaaS dashboard: no cards, no generous whitespace, no summary tiles above the data. Every one of those costs rows.
- NOT a consumer app: no onboarding, no empty-state illustration, no tone of voice. Six people use this and all of them were trained by another coordinator.
- NOT the 2024 refresh: half-finished, imported from a template, and the reason there are three stylesheets. Its remains are not a direction to continue in.
