# Trace — Switchboard

*Produced with trace-protocol v0.1 on 2026-08-14 by cold-reading three screens — the dispatch board, the driver list, and the reassign dialog — with no brief, no access to the team, and no knowledge of what the tool was meant to feel like. This is a description of what four years produced. It is not an instruction.*

**Partial as of 2026-08-16.** The colour reading below describes a board that changed the day after this was written. See the decision `2026-08-16 — Red means undo-in-progress and nothing else`; everything else here still holds.

## In three lines

A tool built by people who were in a hurry, for people who are in a hurry. Nothing is decorated, nothing is explained, and everything is one click from everything else. It reads less like a product than like a workbench that someone has arranged exactly to hand and never tidied.

## Qualities

- **Compressed** — sixty rows visible at once, 11px type, 4px of vertical padding. Nothing has room and nothing appears to want any. Reading it feels like reading a departures board.
- **Unauthored** — three greys within four hex points of each other, two font stacks, buttons in three shapes. Not chaotic; sedimentary. You can date the parts of the screen the way you can date wallpaper.
- **Red-saturated** — red marks late routes, unavailable drivers, the undo window and validation errors. Four meanings, one colour, all on screen simultaneously during a busy hour, at which point the board simply reads as red.
- **Fast** — no transitions, no skeleton states, no optimistic spinners. Actions land the instant they are clicked, which is the single most confident thing about the product and the only quality here that reads as a choice rather than a leftover.
- **Unforgiving** — one dialog in the whole tool. Everything else commits on click, silently.

## What it resembles

- An airport departures board: dense, monospaced in feel, scanned rather than read.
- A 2012 admin template, visible underneath the 2024 paint in the sidebar remnants and the button radii.
- A spreadsheet somebody has been maintaining for years and knows by muscle memory.

## What it visibly avoids

- Any decoration at all. No illustration, no empty-state art, no brand mark above the fold.
- Explanation. There is no help text anywhere in the three screens read.
- Motion. Nothing animates, including things that arguably should, like the row that has just changed.

## The contradiction worth naming

The tool is simultaneously the fastest thing its users have and the least legible thing a newcomer could be handed. Those are the same property. Any change that makes it more legible at the cost of speed will be experienced as damage by the six people who use it, and any change that keeps the speed will keep looking, to everyone else, like something nobody designed.
